FINAL ANSWER
======================================================================================================================
1. The three most valuable skills, holding experience, location and title fixed, are java (+22.8%, 95% CI +16.8% to +29.2%, p < 0.001 = 1.39 years of experience equivalent), business_analysis (+18.7%, CI +12.2% to +25.7%, 1.16 yrs) and sql (+18.2%, CI +13.3% to +23.3%, 1.13 yrs).
2. The gradient boosting cross-check (5-fold CV R2 = 0.538 +/- 0.025) agrees only partly: it also puts business_analysis, sas in its own top 10 (shuffling business_analysis costs 0.0019 of test R2), but the two rankings correlate at just Spearman -0.22 (2/10 top-10 overlap) because permutation importance is sign-blind - it counts a skill that pushes pay DOWN (seo, -11.6%) as just as important and puts rare skills like big_data, project_management, hadoop ahead of java, so only business_analysis and seo are confirmed by both methods, and java - the OLS top pick - is worth 0.0000 R2 to the GB model once title and location are in.
3. 13 of the 30 skills have no real effect (p >= 0.05 and the 95% CI contains 0): spark, data_science, big_data, project_management, market_research, sales, digital_marketing, marketing, business_development, hadoop, data_analysis, accounting, auditing - for these the data cannot separate the coefficient from zero, so their apparent -3%..9% spread is noise, not pay signal.

LIMITATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Observational data: a skill's coefficient is a partial correlation, not a causal ROI - skills co-occur (java with outsourcing/contract roles, sql with senior engineering), hiring teams pick skills they already pay for, and a keyword match in key_skills shows the posting lists the skill, not that the candidate has it.
2. Salary is a band midpoint (0to3, 6to10, ...) so all uplifts are on a coarsened outcome, some skills appear in only ~220-350 postings (wide CIs), the GB permutation importance splits credit unpredictably between correlated skills, and CV R2 = 0.54 leaves about 46% of pay variance unexplained.
