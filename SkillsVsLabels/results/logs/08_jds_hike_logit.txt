======================================================================================================================
FINAL ANSWER
======================================================================================================================
1. The top 2 skills separating high from low hikes are maths_stats (odds ratio 3.8x per 1 SD, 95% CI 1.7x-8.4x, p = 0.001) and storytelling (3.1x, CI 1.6x-6.1x, p = 0.001): in plain words, a junior whose maths stats score is one SD (0.8 points) higher has about 4 times the odds of getting a high hike even when the other four skills are held equal, and the same one-SD edge in storytelling is worth about 3 times the odds.
2. Yes, the model clearly beats guessing: repeated stratified 5-fold CV gives ROC-AUC 0.886 +- 0.068 (random = 0.500) and accuracy 83.6% +- 8.0% against the 50.8% you get by always predicting the majority class, with precision 0.83, recall 0.86 and F1 0.84; the depth-3 tree is weaker (AUC 0.768 +- 0.090) and leans on just maths_stats, ai_and_ml, storytelling.
3. Confidence: with only n = 118 rows and 60 high-hike cases over 5 predictors (12 events per variable, just above the usual 10-per-variable rule of thumb) the direction of the top 2 skills is solid but the magnitudes are imprecise - their CIs run 1.7x-8.4x and 1.6x-6.1x, single splits swing AUC by ~0.07, and coding_skills alone is not significant (p = 0.19); the coding x storytelling interaction is not supported (LRT chi2(1) = 0.99, p = 0.32), so we cannot claim combined effects at this sample size.
